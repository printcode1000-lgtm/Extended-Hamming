The survey connects foundational error-control coding, GDI and FS-GDI logic development, low-power XOR design, and recent encoder implementations. Records are maintained as both a readable synthesis and a structured comparison dataset.

Method and scope

Sources were selected for direct relevance to Hamming/SEC-DED coding, transistor-level XOR architectures, GDI/FS-GDI design, variation-aware low-power evaluation, and reproducibility. Foundational work establishes coding and logic principles; modern sources frame current architectures and reporting expectations. Bibliographic identity and official or publisher locations are recorded where available.

Findings

  • Reduced transistor count alone does not establish energy, timing, swing, or robustness superiority.
  • FS-GDI restoration improves output levels but introduces capacitance and switching overhead.
  • Published encoder comparisons often lack complete netlists, activity patterns, path definitions, or operating-boundary evidence.
  • Encoder-only simulation does not prove SEC-DED correction; a decoder/reference model is required.

Research relationship

This thesis responds with a disclosed PTM model, matched LTspice stimuli, common measurement definitions, full experiment identifiers, exhaustive SEC-DED reference testing, and retained rejected measurements. Novelty remains a provisional, literature-supported contribution pending supervisor and peer evaluation.
