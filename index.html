Abstract

This work evaluates CMOS, GDI, full-swing gate-diffusion-input (FS-GDI), and selective hybrid implementations of Hamming (11,7) and extended Hamming (12,7) encoders. The common LTspice testbench uses a predictive 65-nm bulk-CMOS model and controlled voltage, temperature, frequency, and load sweeps. Exhaustive reference-model tests validate SEC-DED behavior; validated transistor-level results support a speed-oriented hybrid candidate while retaining the lower-energy FS-GDI result.

Current status: Working thesis draft. Simulation and reference-model evidence is available; supervisor approval, institutional front matter, and an optional foundry-PDK or second-simulator cross-check remain pending. No publication, acceptance, fabrication, or silicon-measurement claim is made.

Objectives

  1. Audit and reproduce the published Hamming (11,7) baseline under a disclosed LTspice environment.
  2. Extend the encoder to Hamming (12,7) SEC-DED and verify its Boolean behavior exhaustively.
  3. Compare CMOS, GDI, FS-GDI, and selective-restoration hybrid implementations under matched conditions.
  4. Report power, critical-path delay, PDP, energy, output swing, and transistor-count trade-offs without suppressing unfavorable evidence.

Main contribution

The proposed Hybrid-B architecture retains FS-GDI parity logic while selectively restoring the five externally observed parity outputs with CMOS buffers. At the nominal test condition it reduces power, D1-to-P0 delay, PDP, and device count relative to the CMOS reference. It does not replace FS-GDI as the minimum-power or minimum-PDP option; its contribution is a traceable speed/restoration compromise.
