Abstract

This thesis investigates whether selective CMOS restoration can improve the speed, energy, signal integrity, and operating-condition tolerance of an FS-GDI extended Hamming (12,7) encoder. A published Hamming (11,7) FS-GDI study is independently audited and reproduced using LTspice 26.0.2 and a University of Minnesota PTM 65-nm predictive bulk-CMOS model. Four XOR cells and up to four complete encoder architectures are evaluated under common voltage, temperature, load, and frequency conditions. The extended-code reference model is exhaustively verified for all 128 messages, every single-bit error, and every double-bit error. At the nominal condition, selective-output Hybrid-B is faster than the CMOS and FS-GDI extended encoders, but FS-GDI consumes less power and has the lowest PDP. The evidence therefore supports Hybrid-B as a speed-oriented compromise rather than a universally superior replacement.

1. Introduction

1.1 Problem statement

Error-control encoders are XOR-dominated circuits whose energy and timing depend on logic style, cascade depth, output restoration, and loading. Published low-transistor-count results often omit the operating boundaries needed to determine whether a reduced device count remains useful under voltage and load variation.

1.2 Objectives

  1. Audit and reproduce Hamming (11,7).
  2. Verify extended Hamming (12,7) SEC-DED behavior with a decoder model.
  3. Compare CMOS, GDI, FS-GDI, and limited hybrid candidates fairly.
  4. Quantify power, D1-to-P0 delay, PDP, energy, swing, and hardware complexity.
  5. Map performance under controlled operating variations.

1.3 Contributions

The contribution is a traceable LTspice evaluation framework, a selective-restoration Hybrid-B circuit, correction of numerical inconsistencies in the baseline paper, and an evidence-based trade-off analysis that retains unfavorable results.

2. Literature Survey

Hamming's foundational work establishes the coding theory. GDI reduces device count but may degrade output levels. FS-GDI introduces restoration and has been evaluated under variation in arithmetic circuits. Recent work extends GDI to dynamic, hybrid, and emerging-device cells, while modern ECC research treats SEC-DED and stronger adjacent-error codes. The closest 2024 Hamming study does not provide extended parity, reproducible netlists, or a complete robustness map. The full comparative table is available in survey.csv.

Research gap: a reproducible, one-model comparison of short CMOS, FS-GDI, and selectively restored extended-Hamming encoders with explicit P0-path timing and controlled operating sweeps remains insufficiently documented.

3. Theoretical Background

Data D1-D7 occupy code positions 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, and 11. Even-parity bits occupy positions 1, 2, 4, and 8. The overall parity bit P0 is appended at position 12.

P1 = D1 ⊕ D2 ⊕ D4 ⊕ D5 ⊕ D7 (3.1)
P2 = D1 ⊕ D3 ⊕ D4 ⊕ D6 ⊕ D7 (3.2)
P4 = D2 ⊕ D3 ⊕ D4;   P8 = D5 ⊕ D6 ⊕ D7 (3.3)
P0 = D1 ⊕ ··· ⊕ D7 ⊕ P1 ⊕ P2 ⊕ P4 ⊕ P8 (3.4)

SEC-DED is a property of the complete encoder/decoder system. The encoder alone does not correct errors.

4. Reproduction and Validation of Hamming (11,7)

The published design implies twelve XOR2 instances: four for P1, four for P2, and two each for P4 and P8. The reconstructed CMOS and FS-GDI counts are therefore 144 and 72 transistors, matching the paper. Numerical equality is not expected because the paper used Cadence with an undisclosed foundry deck, while this work uses LTspice and PTM.

5. Extended Hamming (12,7) Architecture

The baseline parity network is extended by a ten-XOR P0 tree that combines the seven data bits and four Hamming parity bits. The same Boolean graph is used for each logic style. Complete encoder comparison, rather than cell-only comparison, is required because cascade depth and fan-out materially change performance.

6. Hybrid FS-GDI/CMOS Design

Hybrid-A places a non-inverting two-inverter CMOS buffer after every FS-GDI XOR, producing 220 transistors. Hybrid-B uses FS-GDI in the parity network and restores only the five externally observed parity outputs, producing 152 transistors. Hybrid-B is selected as the proposed speed-oriented candidate because it avoids most local-buffer overhead while improving the measured P0 path. This selection does not imply minimum energy.

9. Comparative Analysis and Discussion

At nominal conditions, FS-GDI minimizes simulated power and PDP but is slower than CMOS on the deeper P0 path. Hybrid-A restores every cell and largely loses the power advantage. Hybrid-B reduces buffering overhead and gives the shortest D1-to-P0 delay, while retaining materially lower power than CMOS. The cost is substantially higher energy than unbuffered FS-GDI. These observations are consistent with a capacitance-versus-restoration trade-off, although physical causality would require internal-node energy or post-layout evidence.

No architecture failed the chosen functional criteria at 0.6 V in the tested 10 fF voltage sweep. Consequently, this experiment cannot claim a minimum operating voltage below 0.6 V; additional lower-voltage points would be required.

10. Conclusions and Future Work

The project establishes a reproducible transistor-level baseline and demonstrates that selective restoration changes the design trade-off rather than uniformly improving it. Hybrid-B achieved the best nominal critical-path delay and improved power, PDP, and transistor count relative to CMOS. FS-GDI achieved much lower energy and PDP but a longer P0 path. The proposed hybrid is therefore justified only where speed and restored external drive outweigh its energy overhead.

Limitations include the predictive rather than foundry-qualified model, absence of layout parasitics, a reconstructed FS-GDI topology, and a finite activity sequence. Future work should add a second simulator or PDK cross-check, post-layout extraction, lower-voltage boundary search, and statistically controlled device variation.
